**Slug:** `/guides/phase-2-sounds-in-order` · **Pillar:** Phonics & Early Reading

### Top rankers (UK)

|---|------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Phase 2 phonics pages | twinkl.co.uk | Teacher-focused, worksheet-gated; pronunciation guidance thin |
| 2 | School phonics curriculum pages | various .sch.uk | Accurate order but PDF-style, not parent-friendly |
| 3 | "Phase 2 sounds" listicles | various blogs | Order often wrong or incomplete; no pure-sound guidance |

### Content gap
No strong ranking HTML page combines the correct ordered sequence, pure-sound
pronunciation guidance, decodable practice words, a parent-friendly home
routine, and an honest explanation of scheme variation. Top results are
teacher-focused or PDF-based.

### Unique angle
"The parent's pronunciation guide" — lead with the ordered Sets 1–5 in the
first 100 words, then the thing nobody teaches parents: pure sounds
('sss' not 'suh', 'mmm' not 'muh') with a per-sound quick reference.

### Featured-snippet / AEO format
- Answer box (40–60 words): ordered Sets 1–5 list.
- Supporting list: set → sounds → "how to say it purely" → first words to try.

### Verified facts
- Classic Letters and Sounds (DfE, 2007) Phase 2 order — Set 1: s, a, t, p;
  Set 2: i, n, m, d; Set 3: g, o, c, k; Set 4: ck, e, u, r; Set 5: h, b, f, ff,
  ll, ss. Verified against the original DfE Phase Two material and multiple
  UK school/teaching sources.
- Original Phase 2 tricky words: the, to, I, no, go (five). Some newer schemes
  add "into" (six).
- Timing: roughly six weeks, beginning in the first weeks of Reception
  (autumn term). Do NOT claim every school follows the same weekly schedule —
  use "many schools" / "in the original Letters and Sounds sequence".

### Caveats
Schools using current DfE-validated SSP schemes (Little Wandle, Read Write
Inc., Twinkl Phonics) may teach a slightly different grouping. The article
states this honestly and tells parents to follow their school's order for
homework.

## A1 — What is Phonics Phase 2?
**Slug:** `/guides/what-is-phonics-phase-2` · **Pillar:** Phonics & Early Reading

### Top rankers (UK)

|---|------|--------|-------|

| 2 | School curriculum PDFs | various .sch.uk | Accurate but not written for parents |
| 3 | Parent blog posts | various | Thin, often conflate Phase 2 with Phase 3 |

### Content gap
No strong parent-first HTML explainer covering what Phase 2 is, when it
happens, what came before (Phase 1), what comes next (Phases 3–6),
plain-English phonics terminology, and what parents should do at home.

### Unique angle
"The 5-minute parent briefing" — a 40–60-word answer-first definition, then a
full Phases 1–6 timeline so parents see where their child is, plus a
plain-English glossary (phoneme, grapheme, blend, segment, digraph, tricky
word, CVC).

### Featured-snippet / AEO format
- Answer box (40–60 words): direct definition of Phase 2.
- Supporting list: phase timeline (Phase 1 → Phase 6).

### Verified facts
- Phase 2 = first letter-sounds + blending/segmenting CVC words + a handful of
  tricky words; taught in Reception autumn term (~6 weeks).
- Phase 1 (nursery): listening games, no letters. Phase 3 (Reception spring,
  ~12 weeks): remaining letters + digraphs (ch, sh, th, ng) and long vowels
  (ai, ee, igh, oa, oo). Phase 4: longer words, no new sounds. Phase 5
  (Year 1): alternative spellings + June phonics screening check. Phase 6
  (Year 2): spelling rules.
- Year 1 phonics screening check: statutory, June, 40 words (real + pseudo).

### Caveats
Same scheme-variation caveat as A2. Do not present the screening check as
something parents should prepare for — the article frames it as reassurance.

## B1 — Number formation 1–20
**Slug:** `/guides/number-formation-1-to-20` · **Pillar:** Early Maths

### Top rankers (UK)
| # | Page | Domain | Notes |
|---|------|--------|-------|
| 1 | "Practising Number Formation in KS1 (Ages 5–7)" | twinkl.co.uk (blog) | Dominant content ranker. What/Why/How-to + resource links. Aimed at KS1 (5–7), long product-CTA tail. **No per-numeral rhymes, no pencil grip, no reversals.** |
| 2 | "Number Formation Rhymes" (forum thread) | eyfs.info | The classic UK Foundation Stage rhyme set ("Anita's rhymes"). Thin, unstructured, forum-only. |
| 3 | "Number Formation Guide" (school maths leaflet) | simonside.newcastle.sch.uk | Formation via starting dots + arrows; "bad habits are hard to break"; 4 and 5 need pencil lifted. Good school authority, not a full parent guide. |
| 4 | "Number Formation Rhymes 0-9 with Miss Ellis" | YouTube + TES | Widely-used UK rhyme set (video). Strong with teachers; not a text article. |
| 5 | School maths help pages | various UK primaries | One-liners ("write numbers 1–10 using correct formation"), no depth. |

### Content gap
No ranker gives per-numeral formation steps + rhymes for 0–9 (and how teens
are built) in one parent-friendly page for ages 3–6. No ranker covers
pencil-grip stages or reversals with reassurance. Nothing is written for EYFS
parents deciding *when and whether* to start.

### Unique angle
"The honest parent's guide to number formation (EYFS 3–6)" — lead with the
myth-bust (not an EYFS requirement; a handwriting habit; no single "correct"
rhyme set), then deliver what nobody has in one page: the 0–9 rhymes UK
teachers use (attributed), why 11–20 are just two-digit combos, NHS grip
stages by age, reversals-are-normal reassurance, and "match your school".

### Featured-snippet / AEO format
- Answer box (40–60 words): "Number formation is…" definition + first rhymes.
- Ordered list: number-formation rhymes 0–9 (one rhyme per item).
- Small list/table: pencil-grip stages by age.

### Verified facts
- **Number formation is NOT a statutory EYFS requirement.** The EYFS
  statutory framework's maths ELGs cover deep understanding of number to 10,
  subitising to 5, number bonds, and verbal counting beyond 20; the Writing
  ELG covers letters, not numerals. Formation is a motor-consistency /
  legibility aid — schools teach it so numbers are readable.
- **Rhyme set used:** the widely-shared UK primary-school set (as on
  communication4all rhyme cards, consistent with the eyfs.info "Anita's
  rhymes" thread): 0 "Around and round and round we go, when we get home we
  have a zero"; 1 "Start at the top and down we run…"; 2 "Around and back on a
  railroad track, two, two, two"; 3 "Around the tree and around the tree…";
  4 "Down and over, down some more…" (lift pencil); 5 "Down and around, then
  a flag on high…" (lift pencil); 6 "Down we go and make a loop, number six
  makes a hoop"; 7 "Across the sky and down from heaven…"; 8 "Make an 's' and
  do not wait, when it's joined up you have an eight"; 9 "Make a loop and
  then a line…". Wordings vary by school — article says to match the school.
- **Pencil grip stages (NHS — Aneurin Bevan University Health Board paediatric
  OT):** 1–2y palmar supinate; 2–3y digital pronate; 3–4y static quadripod;
  4–6y static tripod; 5–7y+ dynamic tripod. A "correct" tripod is NOT
  expected at 3–4.
- **Reversals are normal** up to around age 7–8; not a sign of dyslexia on
  their own. Practical tip (borrowed from Simonside Primary): underline 6
  and 9 to avoid confusion.

### Caveats
- The exact Writing ELG wording was verified from framework reproductions,
  not the gov.uk page itself — the article avoids quoting it verbatim.
- Do not claim writing 1–20 before Reception is any kind of requirement.

## D1 — Reception readiness checklist UK
**Slug:** `/guides/reception-readiness-checklist-uk` · **Pillar:** School Readiness

### Top rankers (UK)
| # | Page | Domain | Notes |
|---|------|--------|-------|
| 1 | "School Readiness Checklist for Parents" | ptkids.co.uk | Strongest competitor. Myth-bust early, 4 structured areas, warm tone. Weak: therapy-clinic CTA bias; no school-age/deferral facts; no "when to worry / who to call"; doesn't name the 2025 coalition definition. |
| 2 | "Supporting your child to start school" | wchc.nhs.uk | NHS authority but thin and dated. |
| 3 | "Starting School Checklist" | clubhubuk.co.uk | Honest note on the 11–12-month age spread in one class. |
| 4 | "All you need to know about reception" | explorelearning.co.uk | Mixes end-of-Reception expectations into a ready-for-Reception article — confusing, but ranks. |
| 5 | startingreception.co.uk | coalition site | The 2025 definition itself — the source everyone should cite; doesn't rank for the query. |
| 6 | Individual school PDFs | .sch.uk / .gov.uk | Inflate expectations ("write my own name", "blend CVC words") — good contrast material for myth-busting. |

### Content gap
Nobody leads with the big myth-bust (no official test, no pass mark, no entry
requirement). Nobody covers school-age/timing facts (September-after-4th
norm, compulsory school age, deferral/part-time, summer-born rights, the
~12-month age spread). Nobody explains the Reception Baseline Assessment
honestly. Nobody anchors on the actual 2025 consensus definition. Nobody
covers "when to actually worry / who to talk to".

### Unique angle
"Is my child ready for Reception? The honest answer" — open with the
myth-bust in the first 100 words, then the one definition that exists (the
2025 coalition's four pillars in plain English), school-age/deferral facts,
what really happens in the first six weeks, and when to talk to the health
visitor vs relax.

### Featured-snippet / AEO format
- Answer box (40–60 words): "There is no official Reception readiness test…"
- Bulleted list: the four Starting Reception pillars.
- FAQ schema: "Does my child need to read before Reception?" → "No.";
  "Is there a test?" → "Baseline assessment, no pass mark, nothing to prepare."

### Verified facts
- **No statutory readiness checklist or entry test exists.** The DfE/EYFS sets
  no readiness requirements.
- **The 2025 "Starting Reception" definition** (startingreception.co.uk) — a
  coalition of early-years organisations (National Day Nurseries Association,
  Early Years Alliance, Confederation of School Trusts), endorsed at launch
  by the then Education Secretary. Guidance, not pass/fail. Four pillars:
  (1) Growing independence; (2) Building relationships & communicating;
  (3) Physical development; (4) Healthy routines.
- **Reception Baseline Assessment:** statutory since September 2021, taken
  within the first 6 weeks. NOT a pass/fail test — no numerical score shared,
  not used to label individual pupils; DfE uses it only for school-level
  progress measures to end of KS2. Children "should not realise they are
  doing an assessment." Article line: "Your child needs no preparation.
  There is nothing to revise for."
- **School starting age:** most start Reception full-time in the September
  after their 4th birthday. **Compulsory school age** is the term after the
  5th birthday (prescribed days 31 Dec / 31 Mar / 31 Aug; Education Act 1996
  s.8). Parents can defer or go part-time until then; summer-born children
  (1 April–31 August) may request Reception entry at 5 instead of Year 1
  (School Admissions Code). A Reception class can span nearly 12 months of age.

### Caveats
- The claim "a school cannot refuse admission if a child isn't toilet
  trained" was NOT verified from a primary source — **omitted** from the
  article.
- The 90%-parents / 1-in-3-teachers expectation-gap stat is attributed to
  press coverage of the 2025 definition, not presented as our own research.
- "Endorsed by the then Education Secretary" — worded to stay accurate
  regardless of who holds the office now.

## Cross-cutting notes
- **Honest curriculum lines are the differentiator.** Every article leads with
  what parents are NOT required to do, then delivers the genuinely useful
  content. This beats Twinkl-style generic advice and school-PDF wishlists.
- **Uncertainties were omitted, not published.** Two flagged items (exact
  Writing ELG wording; toilet-training/admissions claim) were left out of the
  articles rather than published on assumption.
- Tone: parent-first, zero scare tactics, zero hard sell. One natural product
  CTA per article, at the end.
