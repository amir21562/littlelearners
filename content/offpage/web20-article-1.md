# I Cancelled Our Worksheet Subscription. Here's What We Do Instead.

Every month, £7.99 left our account for a worksheet subscription. Every month,
my four-year-old used about three of the worksheets. The rest sat in a downloads
folder, gathering digital dust.

I did the maths one Sunday evening. Fourteen months subscribed. Roughly £112
spent. Maybe forty worksheets actually printed. That's nearly £3 per worksheet —
for PDFs.

So I cancelled it. Here's what we do now, and why it works better.

## The problem with subscriptions

Subscriptions sell you *access*, not *learning*. The library is enormous, which
sounds great until you realise a four-year-old doesn't need 10,000 worksheets.
They need the same 20 worksheets, repeated until the skill sticks.

Worse, the monthly fee creates a weird pressure. You've paid for it, so you
feel you should use it. Printing becomes a chore instead of a five-minute game
before dinner.

## What we do instead: buy once, print forever

We switched to buying individual printable packs — one payment, the PDF is ours
forever. Our current shelf (a single folder on the printer, honestly) covers:

- **Alphabet tracing** — one page per letter, printed again and again until the
  wobbly B turns into a proper B
- **Phase 2 phonics** — matched to what Reception actually teaches, so home
  practice lines up with school
- **Number formation to 20** — the same pages reprinted every few weeks; you
  can literally see the progress
- **Scissor skills** — cutting practice that costs nothing to repeat

Total cost for all of it: less than three months of the old subscription. And
there's nothing to cancel, pause, or guiltily ignore.

## The 10-minute routine

The worksheets only work because of the routine around them. Ours is dead
simple:

1. **Same time every day** — right after breakfast, before the day gets busy.
2. **One page, not ten** — a single tracing page beats a tired child and a
   stack of half-done sheets.
3. **We sit together** — I drink tea, they trace. My presence is the actual
   teaching; the worksheet is just the excuse.
4. **Repeat, don't rush** — the same letter page three days in a row is not
   failure. It's how motor memory forms.

## What I'd tell any parent

You don't need a subscription to teach a four-year-old. You need a printer,
ten minutes, and the willingness to print the same page twice.
