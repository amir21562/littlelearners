# 7 Rainy-Day Printable Activities for Ages 3–6 (No Prep Needed)

It's raining. The kids are bouncing off the sofa. You have a printer, ten
minutes, and zero energy for crafts involving glitter.

Print one of these. Pour tea. Survive until lunch.

## 1. Letter tracing races

Print a single alphabet tracing page. You trace a letter, your child traces the
next one. First to finish the row wins — then switch letters. Slow, deliberate
tracing beats fast scribbling, so "win" by neatness, not speed.

## 2. Number detectives

Print a numbers-to-20 page and grab a highlighter. Call out a number; your
child hunts it down and highlights it. Then swap roles — let them call the
numbers while you "struggle" to find 17. They will correct you with enormous
satisfaction.

## 3. Sound scavenger hunt

Write five Phase 2 sounds (s, a, t, p, i) on sticky notes and hide them around
the room. Your child finds each one, says the sound, and traces it on a phonics
page. Movement plus phonics beats sitting still every time.

## 4. Cut-and-sort shapes

Print a shapes page, but instead of colouring, cut the shapes out. Then sort
them: "all the round ones here, all the pointy ones there." Cutting builds
hand strength; sorting builds early maths vocabulary. Two skills, one page.

## 5. Colouring with rules

Hand over a colouring page with one constraint: "the cat must be blue, the sun
must be green." Following silly rules is genuinely good practice for classroom
listening — and the results are hilarious enough for the fridge.

## 6. Addition with raisins

Print a simple addition page. Cover each counting dot with a raisin (or a
button, or a Lego brick) as you count it, then eat the evidence. Snack maths
is the best maths.

## 7. The "teach me" game

Let your child "teach" you a tricky word. They trace it, read it, then hold
the page up and make you copy them. Children who teach a skill retain it far
better than children who only practise it — and they take the role extremely
seriously.
